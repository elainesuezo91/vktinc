FIELD NOTE

You don't need an ERP yet. You need the parts of one you'd actually use.

Our CFO·2 min read·November 2026

If you bought an ERP tomorrow, which parts of it would you actually use?

Most growing businesses reach a point where someone says “we need a proper system.” Usually that means an ERP like Odoo, NetSuite or SAP Business One. It also means a project that touches accounting, inventory, purchasing, sales and HR all at once. For a business of 20 or 50 people, that's a big change to make for what are often 3 specific problems.

“Most SMBs don't need an ERP. They need the 3 parts of one that hurt right now.”

The part I pay attention to

When a business tells me it needs an ERP, I ask what went wrong last month. The answer is rarely “our general ledger isn't good enough.” It's closer to this. We sold stock we didn't have. Purchase orders don't match what arrived. Nobody can see a job's cost until it's finished. Those are inventory, procurement and job-costing problems. The accounting platform is usually fine.

WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING
The accounting system works
Stock, purchasing and jobs live in spreadsheets around it
An ERP would replace all of it to fix the middle

What I'd do before buying an ERP

Keep the accounting platform you already trust. Fix the operations around it first:

  • One place for stock and its movements
  • Purchase orders that carry through to receiving
  • Job records that collect costs as they happen

Then connect those to accounting so nothing gets retyped.

That's the part of an ERP you'd actually use, without migrating your books. We build it on AnyDB because we can start with one area and add the next when it's needed. If you still genuinely need a full ERP after that, you'll know exactly why, and you'll go in with clean data.

Fix the parts that hurt. Keep the parts that work.
