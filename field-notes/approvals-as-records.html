FIELD NOTE

“Approved” is a conversation. Make it a record.

Our CFO·2 min read·November 2026

When finance asks who approved a purchase, how long does it take to find out?

In most small and mid-sized businesses, approval happens in a chat thread, a forwarded email, or a quick word at someone's desk. It's fast and it feels fine. Then month-end comes, and accounting has an invoice in one hand and no idea whose “go ahead” it belongs to.

“An approval nobody can find later isn't an approval. It's a memory.”

The part I pay attention to

When I'm closing the books, the delays rarely come from the numbers. They come from the questions around the numbers. Was this purchase order approved before it was placed? Was it approved at this amount, or the earlier quote? Who agreed to the extra delivery charge? Each answer exists somewhere, usually in someone's phone. Someone in accounting has to go and find it.

WHAT'S ACTUALLY HAPPENING
Approvals happen in chat, email and conversation
The purchase, the invoice and the “yes” live in different places
Accounting rebuilds the link by asking around

What I'd do before tightening the rules

Don't add more approvers. After a bad month, most businesses respond by asking more people to sign off, which just makes the chat threads longer. The faster fix is to make the approval part of the record. That means the request, the amount, who approved it and when, all attached to the purchase order the invoice will later match.

That doesn't need a heavyweight workflow system. It needs a request form, a simple rule for who approves what amount, and the approval stored next to the PO. We build that in AnyDB for clients who've outgrown email approvals. Some get there first with a simpler form and a shared tracker. What matters is that the “yes” can be found without asking anyone.

If you have to ask who approved it, it wasn't recorded.

Where AI fits

Once the approval is a record, AI can do the chasing: route the request to the right approver, send the reminder, and flag a PO with no approval before the invoice arrives. The decision stays with a person. AI just stops it from sitting in an inbox.
